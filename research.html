Research

My research connects atmospheric science, machine learning, and climate-sensitive health to understand a changing climate and its implications for India. I combine climate simulations, observations, and statistical optimization to improve regional projections, investigate extreme heat, and examine long-term monsoon variability.

I also bring climate data together with demographic information and mosquito ecology to investigate changing malaria transmission risks. Across these projects, I connect physical climate processes with computational methods and health impacts, aiming to produce reliable information for adaptation, early warning, and public health planning.

Projects

Advancing Reliable Climate Projections for India

Research Areas: Climate Modeling | CMIP6 | Machine Learning | Statistical Optimization

Global climate models provide essential insights into future climate change, yet substantial biases and uncertainties in their simulations often limit their reliability for regional climate assessments and policy decisions. This research aims to enhance the accuracy and robustness of climate projections over India by leveraging the extensive archive of climate simulations from the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project (CMIP).

By integrating advanced machine learning techniques, statistical optimization, and observation-constrained approaches, the study seeks to identify and reduce systematic model biases across key climate variables. The broader objective is to transform complex climate model outputs into more reliable, regionally relevant climate information that can support evidence-based policymaking, climate adaptation, and long-term resilience planning.

Enhancing Heatwave Prediction over the Indo-Gangetic Plain

Research Areas: Extreme Heat | Machine Learning | Random Forest | XGBoost | Climate Predictability

The Indo-Gangetic Plain (IGP) is highly vulnerable to extreme heat events, posing significant risks to public health, agriculture, and socioeconomic activities. Improving the prediction of these events is therefore essential for strengthening early warning systems and mitigating heat-related impacts.

This research explores the application of machine learning algorithms, including Random Forest and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), to improve the prediction of maximum surface air temperature across the region. By incorporating atmospheric and oceanic predictors such as wind patterns, outgoing longwave radiation (OLR), and sea surface temperature (SST), the study investigates complex relationships between large-scale climate drivers and regional temperature variability. The ultimate objective is to develop more accurate and robust heatwave prediction frameworks that can contribute to effective heat-risk preparedness and management.

Projecting Climate-Driven Changes in Malaria Distribution across India

Research Areas: Climate Change & Health | Vector-Borne Diseases | VECTRI | Malaria Transmission Modeling

Climate change has the potential to alter the geographical distribution and transmission dynamics of vector-borne diseases, including malaria. Understanding these changes is critical for identifying emerging health risks and supporting targeted disease prevention strategies.

This research employs the VECTRI (Vector-borne Disease Community Model of ICTP) framework to investigate potential shifts in malaria transmission suitability across India under changing climatic conditions. The model integrates daily precipitation and temperature data with demographic information, including population density, and entomological characteristics of mosquito vectors to simulate malaria transmission dynamics.

A key indicator examined in the study is the Entomological Inoculation Rate (EIR), which represents the number of infectious mosquito bites received per person per unit time. By analyzing projected changes in EIR and associated transmission patterns, the research aims to identify regions that may experience increasing or decreasing malaria transmission risk, providing insights relevant to climate-sensitive public health planning.

Understanding Long-Term Trends and Variability in the Indian Summer Monsoon

Research Areas: Indian Summer Monsoon | Climate Variability | Historical Rainfall | Trend Analysis

The Indian summer monsoon is a fundamental component of the regional climate system, sustaining agricultural productivity, water resources, and the livelihoods of millions. Understanding its long-term evolution and spatial variability is essential for assessing regional climate change and its implications.

This research presents a comprehensive analysis of historical Indian summer monsoon rainfall over the past century, with particular emphasis on its trends and variability across India's homogeneous rainfall regions. By examining regional differences in rainfall behavior and long-term temporal changes, the study seeks to characterize the spatial heterogeneity of monsoon variability and identify patterns of increasing or decreasing precipitation.

The findings aim to advance our understanding of historical monsoon behavior and provide a scientific foundation for evaluating future changes in India's regional rainfall regimes.
